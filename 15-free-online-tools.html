15 Free Online Tools I Use Every Day

By gulam

I fix small digital chores every single day — merging PDFs for paperwork, compressing screenshots for a blog post, counting words before I hit publish, generating a strong password, or checking a meta description. For years I hopped between a dozen different sites, each with a sign-up wall, a watermark, or a “three free uses a month” limit. So I built myself a shortlist of tools that are simply free, with no account required.

Here are the 15 free online tools I reach for most:

  1. PDF merger — combining invoices or receipts into one tidy file.
  2. PDF compressor — shrinking a file before emailing it.
  3. Image compressor — cutting down photo sizes for faster web pages.
  4. Image resizer — quick dimensions for social posts.
  5. Background remover — cleaning up product shots.
  6. Word counter — keeping articles within length.
  7. Character counter — for meta titles and social captions.
  8. Case converter — fixing ALL-CAPS pasted text.
  9. JSON formatter — reading API responses like a human.
  10. Password generator — strong, random passwords in one click.
  11. QR code generator — links you can print or stick on a flyer.
  12. URL encoder/decoder — untangling messy query strings.
  13. Meta tag generator — SEO basics for a new page.
  14. Percentage calculator — discounts, tips, and margins.
  15. Age calculator — quicker than doing the maths by hand.

Most of these live in one place now: ToolVerse US, a free collection of more than 1,000 online tools that runs entirely in the browser. I maintain it, and the rule is simple — every tool is free, with nothing to install and no account to create.

That is the real test I apply before bookmarking anything: does it solve the job in under a minute, and does it ask for anything in return? If the answer to the second question is “yes”, it does not make the list. The fifteen above pass both tests, and together they save me a surprising amount of time every single week — probably an hour or more that used to disappear into sign-up forms and paywalls.
